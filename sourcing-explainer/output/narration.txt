Scene 0, what the CFO wants
Every CFO wants two things. No surprises. And spend that moves the business forward.

Scene 1, the promise
So here is the promise: no surprises, faster yeses, and every yes backed by data.

Scene 2, A faster yes
First, a faster yes. A product team needs Vendor Y, a fictional data partner, to launch. A typical sourcing cycle takes 12 weeks. With reviews and a shortlist ready before the ask, it takes 4 weeks. The launch ships 8 weeks earlier. That is 8 more weeks of revenue.

Scene 3, No surprises
Next, no surprises. Vendor Z, also fictional, supports ad delivery. Usage is at 85 percent of capacity. The vendor was just acquired. Peak season is 120 days out. Alone, each signal is noise. Together, they say act now. The team locks in capacity and a backup before the peak.

Scene 4, Caught in the draft
And no leakage. Vendor X's renewal draft raises the price cap from 2 percent to 6 percent. On 1 million dollars of spend, that is 40,000 dollars a year, caught in the draft, not on the invoice. The move: restore the 2 percent cap before renewal.

Scene 5, Backed by data
Every yes is backed by data. Each number links to the record it came from. The cycle time comes from the sourcing log. The capacity risk comes from usage data. The 40,000 dollars comes from the contract draft. No source, no number.

Scene 6, How AI fits in
Here is how AI fits in. One portfolio holds more than 150 vendors, with signals across five systems. No person can watch them all. AI agents read every contract, renewal, review, and news item. They score each signal and cite the source. They draft the brief and the next move. A person makes the call.
